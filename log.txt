In the case study, Deadline Monotonic achieves schedulability ratios comparable to OPA, because DNNs with higher parallelism levels typically have longer execution times, and consequently, larger deadlines. As a result, the Deadline Monotonic algorithm naturally assigns lower priorities to tasks with higher parallelism levels, which aligns with the intuition behind sorting tasks by their parallelism levels in Algorithm 4. VIII. C ONCLUSION This paper presents new response time analysis techniques and an optimal priority assignment policy for global NP-FP rigid gang scheduling. We propose three schedulability tests based on the concept of the problem window and carry-in interference limitation, which we formulate as generalized knapsack prob- lems. Additionally, we introduce a generalized optimal priority assignment policy that resolves a conflict between our proposed schedulability tests and the compatibility conditions of Auds- ley’s OPA algorithm. The effectiveness of the proposed response time analysis and optimal priority assignment was evaluated through experiments on both synthetic task sets and real-world DNN benchmarks executed on Edge TPUs. The results demon- strate that our approaches significantly improve schedulability compared to state-of-the-art methods for global NP-FP gang scheduling. Furthermore, an ablation study was conducted to assess the impact of different algorithm components, providing valuable insights for future research. We note that although we use Edge TPU as a motivating example and case study in this paper, the proposed techniques can also be applied to other processing units as long as the system model defined in Section III-A is applicable (e.g., FPGAs [7] and GPUs [8]).
